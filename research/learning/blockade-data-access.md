# Access to awake-paralysis EEG for retrospective research

Corresponding authors and data availability are listed in the [Halder et al. study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13612226/) and the [Schuller et al. study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26174308/). This draft can be adapted for each group.

**Subject** Request for de-identified EEG and event markers from your awake neuromuscular blockade study

Dear Professor or Dr [name],

I am a consultant anaesthetist and intensivist in the UK developing MindGuard, a research prototype for interpreting EEG during anaesthesia. I would like to test a specific failure mode observed in your work: an algorithm classifying an awake, paralysed person as more deeply anaesthetised when cranial muscle activity diminishes.

Would your team consider providing controlled access to the original de-identified, numerically sampled EEG from your study? Of particular interest are the pre-block awake period, confirmed awake paralysis, and any available hypnotic administration, reversal and recovery periods. We would welcome the available montage, reference, units and acquisition metadata, synchronised medication and event annotations, and timing of isolated-forearm responses, EMG or neuromuscular monitoring, together with any limitations on the interpretation of those labels.

Our first analysis would freeze the algorithm before testing an independent cohort and measure the proportion of known awake-paralysed volunteers falsely classified as sedated. We would report participants as the unit of inference, document exclusions and protocol deviations, and share aggregate results with the investigators.

Please could you advise what approvals, data-use agreement, access environment and attribution you require? We would also like to clarify the permitted scope for retrospective evaluation, model training, retention of fitted parameters, and potential future commercial medical-device development. We can use the institution's approved transfer mechanism. We would also appreciate confirmation of overlap with related publications to avoid counting the same volunteers twice.

Kind regards,

Rob Coe
